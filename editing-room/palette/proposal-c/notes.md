# Proposal C: professional-first

Generated by `build.py`. Every number below is measured, not typed.

## Colour theory

- One anchor hue: the logo blue, OKLCH hue 239. Everything else is placed relative to it.
- Neutrals sit at hue 250, 11 degrees past the logo blue. At low lightness hue 239 drifts toward petrol. Hue 250 keeps the page navy.
- Neutral chroma is low and rises as lightness falls (0.010 at the top, 0.031 at the bottom). Light greys look grey. Dark greys look navy. Nothing looks dirty.
- Amber sits at hue 66, near the true complement of the logo blue (59). Complements give the strongest figure against ground. We stop 7 degrees short of orange so it never reads as an alarm.
- Green (158) and red (29) are status hues only. Red is 9 degrees off the logo crimson but much lighter in dark mode and used only for errors. Crimson never appears in the UI.
- All ramps share one lightness ladder: 50=0.975, 100=0.94, 200=0.88, 300=0.81, 400=0.73, 500=0.635, 600=0.535, 700=0.455, 800=0.375, 900=0.295, 950=0.215. So `blue-300` and `amber-300` have the same weight. Chroma peaks mid-ramp and tapers to the ends, then is clipped to sRGB by chroma only.
- Logo blue is `blue-600`, exact, and step 600 sits at the logo blue lightness (0.535). Links and info use the same hue: `blue-300` on dark, `blue-700` on light. The logo blue itself passes on the plain light page (4.69:1) but drops to 4.30:1 on the info tint, so text takes one step darker.
- Balance is 60/30/10. 60: navy (or near-white) neutrals. 30: blue family (links, info, blue drawings, cool glows). 10: amber, kept for the one thing to click and for highlighted drawing faces.
- Chroma is restrained in the UI: text and surfaces stay under 0.035. The drawings keep full ramp chroma (amber 0.15, green 0.12) so the Overwatch warmth survives where it belongs.
- Light mode is designed, not inverted. Page is `neutral-50`, cards are white glass, dialogs are white. Amber text would fail on white, so the light accent is `amber-600`, a deep burnt amber that works as text and as a fill with white labels.

| light | blue fill | `#005D8A` | `#FFFFFF` | 7.16 | -89 | 6.65 | 0.527 | 11 deg | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Verdict: amber stays the primary. Reasons:
- Isolation. The page is blue-tinted. A blue button sits about 10 degrees from the page hue. Amber sits about 180 degrees away. The amber fill is also far brighter against the dark page (see "Fill vs page"). One amber button wins the eye with no extra size or motion.
- Roles. Blue already means "link" and "info" and is the logo. A blue primary would give blue two jobs. The amber "highlight" left over would have no job. That breaks rule 4.
- Dark mode. The darkest blue that carries a white label (the logo blue) is only about 3:1 against the navy page. A light blue fill passes, but it then looks like the link colour (small dE vs link).
- Professionalism comes from restraint, not from the hue. Rule: one amber fill per view. Everything else is a ghost button, a link, or blue.
- Cost: under tritanopia amber and blue lean toward each other. They stay apart in lightness, and the button has a fill and a label, so it is still found.

Findings:
- Lightness carries the status colours on the page. Hue alone is never trusted.
- Every status message has a glyph (tick, i, !) and words. The online chip has a dot. Errors also get a border.
- Amber and alert red sit close under protan and deutan. Amber never means status: it is only for actions. Alerts always carry the "!" glyph, the word and a border.
- Light ok is `green-800`, darker than alert `red-600`. Protanopes see red darker than it is, so the green has to sit well below the red in lightness or the two merge.

## Known weaknesses

- `--c-accent` does two jobs in light mode: text and button fill. One deep amber (`amber-600`) does both. It reads more burnt-orange than the dark-mode amber. A future `--c-accent-text` token would let light buttons use a brighter amber fill with dark labels.
- `--c-line-2` was raised to meet 3:1 for control edges. Places that used it as a soft card border will look heavier. Those should move to `--c-line`.
- `--c-sky` is the left face of blue boxes. In light mode it is `blue-700`, so blue boxes are much darker than in dark mode. They still read top-light, right-dark.
- Alert red sits 9 degrees from the logo crimson. Light alert is `red-600` (dE 0.105 from crimson). `red-700` was only 0.032 away, so it stays out of the UI.
- The amber box in light mode is close to the white page in lightness. The amber-700 edge and the drop shadow separate it.
- Light-mode `--iso-shadow` is navy, not black, used at 0.35. That may be heavy on big drawings. Check at hero scale.
- APCA Lc for muted text near 60 means: fine at 16px and up, not for 12px labels. Keep 12px labels in `--c-text`.
- `--c-glass` assumes the content under the header is mostly page colour. Over a bright drawing the text ratio drops.
