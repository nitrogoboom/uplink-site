Footer · phones · round 1

Tap here, see it here

On a phone the footer's tabs sit above the map, and the links they open sit below it. Each tap means scrolling down to see what changed and back up to tap again. Three ways to put the result next to the tap, beside the footer as it is now.

  • Tap a section in any phoneEach starts on Support, the way your screenshot did.
  • Scroll inside a phoneThe tab bar hides as you scroll down and comes back as you scroll up, like the site.
  • Read the line under itHow far the first link lands below your tap, and how many links you can see without scrolling.
Now

As it is today

Measuring…

Tabs in one sideways row, then the map, then the links.

  • The links land about 540 px under the tap: below the screen on most phones.
  • Three and a half of six tabs fit. Resources and Follow are off the right edge.
  • The map takes about 290 px of a 715 px screen.
A

Links first, map last

Measuring…

All six tabs in a 3 × 2 grid. The links sit right under them. The map closes the footer.

  • Every tab on screen, and the links show where you tapped.
  • Smallest change: phone CSS, plus dropping the tile's "4 links" line (the panel already says it).
  • The map flies while it's off screen. You only see where it landed when you scroll down.

Change: small. footer.css only (phone rules).

B

Map beside the title

My pick

Measuring…

The same grid, and a small map window next to the section title. One look shows the map fly and the links.

  • Tap, map and links on one screen. The tap still feels like picking a spot.
  • The small map holds still between flights. Nothing loops, so no pause button is needed, and it costs the phone less.
  • The window is small: the lit spot reads, the campus less so. At 200% text it moves under the title.

Change: medium. footer.css, and a few lines in footer.js to move the map into the open panel's heading on phones.

C

Open in place

Measuring…

Six rows under a short map strip. A tap opens that section's links right under the row.

  • The links open under your finger. Works at any text size, never scrolls sideways.
  • Loses the "pick a hero" feel on phones. The map is above the rows and often off screen when it moves.
  • Lower rows open near the bottom edge, so their links can still fall off screen.

Change: larger. Tabs on tablet and desktop, open-in-place rows on phones: footer.js has to run both.

Why B

It fixes the gap and keeps the idea. The footer is a hero select: you pick a section and the camera flies to its spot. B keeps that moment on screen, right beside the result. A fixes the gap too but hides the flight; C fixes it best but turns the footer into a plain list on phones.

If the small map feels too small on your phone, A is the fallback: same grid, same links, map at the end. Going from B to A later is a CSS change.

Same in all three: only phones (under 700 px) change. Tablet and desktop keep today's footer. The tabs stay real tabs (arrow keys, screen-reader names) in A and B.

Looked at and dropped

  • Scroll down for you after a tapTwo lines of script, but the page jumps on every tap and the map flight scrolls away before you see it.
  • Tabs pinned to the bottom of the screenThe game look, and in thumb reach. But it fights the site's tab bar for the same edge, and the page jumps as sections change height.
  • Drop the map on phonesFixes the gap for free, but the map is the footer's one memorable thing.
